Microsoft Sentinel SOC Interview Playbook Threat Hunting Interview Question Bank

Open in Claude; only you can see them.

1. Present yourself in 60 seconds Every interview

"Tell me about yourself" is your first impression. Use four parts, about 15 seconds each:

  1. Who you are: role and years. "I'm a SOC analyst with 2 years in a 24x7 SOC."
  2. Your tools: only the ones in their job. "I work daily in Microsoft Sentinel and Defender for Endpoint, and write KQL."
  3. One proof with a number: "I tuned 12 noisy rules and cut false positives by about 40%."
  4. Why them: "I want to move into threat hunting, and your team runs hunts weekly."
Avoid
  • Reading your CV aloud, or listing every tool you have ever touched.
  • Saying "I'm passionate about security" without a story that shows it.
2. How to answer tricky "what would you do if..." questions L1 to L3

Interviewers care more about how you think than the final answer. Say your steps out loud in this order:

  1. Clarify: ask one or two questions. "Is this a server or a laptop? Is the user an admin?"
  2. Scope: how many users or machines, and how bad (data, money, downtime).
  3. Evidence: name the exact logs you would open (sign-in logs, EDR process tree, proxy, email logs).
  4. Contain: the smallest action that stops the harm: disable the account, isolate the host, block the hash.
  5. Fix and recover: remove the cause, reset credentials, restore.
  6. Tell and write: who you inform (L2, manager, IT) and what goes in the ticket.
"The same user logs in from Dubai and Mumbai 10 minutes apart."Impossible travel. Check whether one IP is a VPN or cloud proxy, whether MFA passed, and whether the device is known. If the login looks real and unknown: revoke the sessions, reset the password, check the mailbox for new forwarding rules, and look for other users signing in from that IP.
"An alert fired 500 times overnight. What do you do?"Don't close 500 tickets one by one. Group by host, user and rule to find the single cause. Check one sample properly: if it's a true positive, treat it as one incident; if it's noise, tune the rule (exclusion or threshold) and record why.
"The CEO's laptop is flagged and his assistant says ignore it."Be polite but follow the process: no one is exempt. Escalate to your lead, investigate quickly and quietly, and explain the risk in business words. VIPs are the most targeted users.
"You don't know the answer."Say so, then say how you'd find it: "I haven't used QRadar's AQL, but in Sentinel I'd do it like this, and I'd check the AQL guide." Honesty plus a method scores better than guessing.
3. SOC analyst L1 / L2 What they test
  • Triage: true or false positive, how bad, who to tell.
  • Basics: TCP/UDP, common ports, DNS, Windows event IDs 4624 (logon), 4625 (failed logon), 4688 (process created), 4720 (user created).
  • Clear incident write-ups.
"Walk me through a phishing alert."Check the sender, headers and links in a sandbox; find everyone who received it (mail logs); find who clicked (proxy logs); block the sender, URL and attachment hash; reset passwords for anyone who typed credentials; write the timeline in the ticket.
"SIEM vs EDR?"A SIEM collects logs from everything and connects the dots (the building's CCTV room). EDR watches and can act on one computer's processes (a guard at one door). Good SOCs use both.
"True positive vs false positive?"A true positive is a real attack that the alert caught. A false positive is an alert on normal activity. The dangerous one is a false negative: a real attack with no alert.
4. Microsoft Sentinel and KQL L2
"Failed sign-ins per user in the last day." 
SigninLogs
| where TimeGenerated > ago(1d)
| where ResultType != "0"
| summarize Failures = count() by UserPrincipalName
| order by Failures desc
Also asked
  • Analytics rule vs hunting query: a rule runs on a schedule and creates incidents; a hunting query is run by you to look around.
  • Playbooks are Logic Apps that act on an incident: enrich an IP, disable a user, post to Teams.
  • Data connectors, Log Analytics workspace, and how you would cut ingestion cost.
5. Splunk and QRadar L1 / L2
Splunk: failed Windows logons by account 
index=wineventlog EventCode=4625
| stats count by Account_Name, src_ip
| sort - count
QRadar: failed logins by user (AQL) 
SELECT username, COUNT(*) AS failures
FROM events
WHERE QIDNAME(qid) ILIKE '%fail%'
GROUP BY username
ORDER BY failures DESC
LAST 24 HOURS
Also asked
  • QRadar: offense vs event, rules and building blocks, log sources and DSMs, what EPS (events per second) licensing means.
  • Splunk: indexes, sourcetypes, forwarders, and the difference between stats and table.
6. EDR / XDR and CrowdStrike L2
  • Process tree: read the parent and child chain. "winword.exe spawning powershell.exe" is a classic bad sign.
  • IOC vs IOA: an IOC is evidence left behind (hash, IP, domain); an IOA is suspicious behaviour while it happens (credential dumping). CrowdStrike Falcon leans on IOAs.
  • Containment: network-isolate the host (it still talks to the EDR console), then use Real Time Response to collect files or kill processes.
"EDR blocked a file. Are you done?"No. Find how it arrived (email, USB, download), whether it ran anywhere else, and whether anything persisted (scheduled tasks, run keys, new services).
7. SOAR and automation L2 / L3
  • Automate the boring, repeatable steps: enrich IPs, hashes and domains, open tickets, notify, and block known-bad indicators.
  • Keep a human on risky actions (disabling an executive's account, isolating a production server).
  • Tools: Cortex XSOAR, Splunk SOAR (Phantom), Sentinel playbooks (Logic Apps), Shuffle. Python and PowerShell basics help a lot.
"Design a phishing playbook."Trigger: user-reported email. Steps: extract URLs and attachments, check reputation and sandbox them, search for other recipients, auto-delete if malicious, block the sender, notify the user, and close with a summary. A human approves deletion when it affects more than 50 mailboxes.
8. Threat hunting L2 / L3
  • Start from a hypothesis: "An attacker uses PsExec to move between servers."
  • Map it to MITRE ATT&CK (T1021.002, SMB/Windows Admin Shares).
  • Query the data, separate normal admin use from the strange, and turn every good hunt into a detection rule.
9. Incident response L2 / L3
  • NIST phases: prepare, detect and analyse, contain, eradicate, recover, lessons learned.
  • Containment trade-off: pulling a server stops the attacker but also the business. Say who decides.
  • Evidence: collect memory before rebooting, keep a timeline, and hash what you collect.
"Ransomware note found on a file server at 2 am."Isolate the server (and its backups) from the network, don't power it off, find patient zero from EDR and logins, check whether data left the network, wake the IR lead and follow the runbook, then restore from clean backups.
10. HR and manager round All
  • Shifts: most SOCs run 24x7. Say clearly whether you can do nights.
  • Notice period: India often 30 to 90 days; UAE usually 30 days. Know yours.
  • Salary: give a range based on research, not your current pay. UAE packages are usually quoted monthly; India packages are quoted yearly (CTC).
  • Visa (UAE): say whether you need sponsorship or already hold a residence visa.
  • "Tell me about a mistake": pick a real one, then what you changed so it can't happen again.

How it works

  1. Every 6 hours your laptop runs E:\JobSearch\run.cmd. It searches LinkedIn for 16 cyber job titles (incl. QRadar, CrowdStrike, SOAR, security administrator) in the UAE and India (jobs from the last 7 days).
  2. It opens each new job once to read the description, then works out the level, years asked and skill tags.
  3. It removes jobs that are not cybersecurity: chip design "SoC" jobs and security guard jobs (see the list in "Last update - sites").
  4. The same job found by several searches or sites is shown once.
  5. A job not seen for 14 days gets a "may be closed" badge; after 30 days it leaves the page.
  6. Your marks (applied, not interested), skills and filters are saved only in this browser.

Why only LinkedIn?

Indeed, Bayt, GulfTalent, NaukriGulf, Naukri and Foundit refuse automated searches from this laptop (403, captcha or no answer). We don't work around that. Their jobs from the 16 Nov web-chat snapshot are kept, marked with their site name, until they age out.
